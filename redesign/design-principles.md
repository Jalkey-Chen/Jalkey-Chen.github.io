# Design Principles

## 1. Editorial Before Decorative

The homepage should read like an edited intellectual introduction. Layout, motion, and visual texture should support the reader's understanding of Peiyu's research presence. Avoid elements that exist only to make the page look "designed."

## 2. Credibility Arrives Early

The first viewport must establish Peiyu Chen, current position, institutional affiliation, research areas, and practical navigation paths. The opening line can be poetic, but the visitor should not wait long to understand who the site is for.

## 3. Questions Over Categories

Avoid default academic labels when they flatten the work. "Research Interests" is weaker than a section organized around questions. "About Me" is weaker than "Formation" or "What shaped my questions."

## 4. Warm Minimalism

Use a warm off-white or paper-like background, generous whitespace, careful typography, and restrained contrast. The site should feel calm and humane, not sterile.

## 5. Typography Carries Atmosphere

Use a modern serif for the opening line, major names, and editorial moments. Use a clean sans-serif for navigation, metadata, buttons, dates, and labels. The serif gives voice; the sans-serif gives clarity.

## 6. Motion Has A Job

Animation should clarify sequence, not entertain. The hero sequence is:

1. Opening line appears.
2. It shifts upward and becomes slightly smaller.
3. Professional identity fades in.
4. Navigation actions fade in.
5. A subtle scroll indicator appears.

Motion should be smooth, calm, and short. Always support reduced-motion preferences.

## 7. Spacious But Not Empty

Whitespace should create attention and pacing. It should not make the page feel underdeveloped. Each section needs enough structure to tell readers where they are and why the content matters.

## 8. Understatement Over Novelty

Avoid flashy visual idioms: gradients, orbs, cyberpunk color, glass effects, particles, 3D scenes, aggressive hover states, and excessive cards. A strong line of text, a clean grid, and subtle texture are usually enough.

## 9. Rebuild The Homepage, Preserve The System

The redesign is not an iteration on the old homepage's look. Existing content can be replaced or reorganized. However, preserve the Jekyll build system, routing conventions, reusable assets, and existing pages unless a later task explicitly asks to change them.

## 10. Maintainability Matters

Future sections should be easy to update. News entries, current work, writing, and CV links should not require fragile layout edits. Prefer clear markup and scoped CSS over clever abstractions.
