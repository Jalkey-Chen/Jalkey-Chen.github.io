# Redesign Constitution

This branch rebuilds Peiyu Chen's homepage from scratch, section by section. It preserves the site's infrastructure, routing, build system, dependencies, and reusable assets where appropriate, but it does not preserve the old homepage's visual logic.

The homepage is an editorial academic experience, not a collection of decorative web components. Every section should help a serious reader understand Peiyu's intellectual position, current credibility, and research questions.

## Core Standard

Professional first, poetic second.

The site should quickly answer:

- Who is this person?
- What institution are they connected to?
- What kind of research do they do?
- Where can I find current work, writing, news, and CV?

After that, it should quietly suggest:

- What questions does this person keep returning to?
- What shaped their research taste?
- Why does the work have human, social, and institutional stakes?

## Audience

The primary audience includes professors, research collaborators, academic readers, and intellectually adjacent visitors. The site should reassure readers who need credibility first, then reward readers who stay longer.

## Conceptual Anchor

The homepage opens with:

"I study the human questions behind technological change."

This is not a slogan. It is the organizing premise of the homepage. Later sections should make this line feel earned through structure, content, and pacing.

## What The Site Should Feel Like

- A quiet editorial academic website
- A thoughtful research presence
- A professional but personal intellectual map
- A site organized around questions, not only credentials
- A page that establishes credibility quickly and then invites deeper reading

## What The Site Should Avoid

- Generic portfolio templates
- Startup landing page aesthetics
- Personal memoir structure
- Heavily animated creative portfolio conventions
- Decorative academic CV layouts
- Lists of research areas with no intellectual framing
- Purple gradients, cyberpunk visuals, glassmorphism, particles, or 3D effects
- Large portrait-led hero sections
- Stock-looking imagery or random institutional decoration

## Decision Rule

When a design or content choice is uncertain, choose the option that makes the site more intellectually precise, quieter, and more credible. Distinctiveness should come from framing, typography, rhythm, and editorial judgment rather than visual spectacle.
